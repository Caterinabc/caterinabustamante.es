Tecno Fast Rental

I redesigned the quote flow for Tecno Fast Rental, a company that rents and sells modular spaces to other businesses. The quote form had stopped receiving requests. I found 17 quote buttons on a single screen leading to different places, and unified them into a single quote cart.

The problem

Tecno Fast's clients don't buy with one click. They request a quote and a sales executive builds the proposal. That request is the entry point of the whole business. The client reported that quote requests had stopped arriving and asked me to check the form for a bug. The site didn't track where requests were lost, so I went through it the way a customer would.

The listing had 17 quote buttons on one screen, in six different types, all for the same action. I followed each one and found they led to three different forms:

Clarity

Where attention goes

Microsoft Clarity's predictive heatmaps use AI to estimate where attention would go from an image or a URL. They don't use real visitor data, so they don't measure actual behavior. I used them because the site didn't track the quote journey. They showed how many focal points competed with each other, and that the problem was bigger than it looked.

The problem was structural. Fixing each form separately would have been faster, but it would have left the cause in place, so I proposed unifying the flow. To support the proposal, I built a presentation that showed the 17 quote buttons and the three forms in a single view. Seeing them together, the client immediately recognized a problem she hadn't noticed before.

Two customers

A questionnaire for the sales team showed that requests didn't come from a single type of customer.

Knows what they needNeeds guidance
ExampleConstruction and industrial companiesCompanies with a project but no defined product
Arrives withSpecific productsA short project description, or nothing
VolumeA minority of requestsThe majority
NeedsCatalog and cartA sales executive to guide them

DecisionOne form serves both. If the customer added products, the summary appears next to the form. If not, the empty cart tells them they can describe their project in the form, and a button takes them to the listing if they'd rather pick products. Customers who need guidance can send a request without going through the catalog.

The proportions come from the sales team's perception, not from direct observation of users.

One cart

Every product card and product page has one action: Agregar a la cotización (Add to quote). Everything leads to a single form, with the product summary in view. Products travel with the request, so the sales executive knows what to quote before calling. The quote button in the header shows how many products the customer has added.

On the product page, specs, floor plan and 3D tour sit in the same view as the quote action. In consultative sales, a customer who understands what they're asking for sends a more useful request.

Cross-sellingA module is almost never quoted alone. The Módulo Protegido insurance appears as an add-on on the product page, and furniture packs are offered next to compatible modules. The quote grows in value without adding steps.

What happened next

Development and sales approved the proposal, but it wasn't implemented, because the final decision rested with someone who wasn't part of the process. Today I would map everyone who makes decisions from the kickoff.

If it gets implemented, I would measure requests received per week and the share that arrive with products, and test the form with both buyer types before launch.
